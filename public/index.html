Your RAM, in plain terms
Wired

Memory pinned in RAM — macOS can never page it out, compress or swap it. Mostly the kernel and IOKit: GPU, display and audio buffers (WindowServer, video, ML/AI compute), plus anything an app locks down.

Freeing it: it can't be purged — the kernel's share clears on a reboot, the apps' share when they quit.

Compressed

Your apps' own unused memory, packed. When an app's pages sit idle, macOS compresses them in RAM instead of writing SSD swap.

Freeing it: quit the app or close its tabs — and it's never pressure by itself, macOS uncompresses on demand.

File cache

Disk data cached for fast reads. macOS reclaims it automatically the moment any app needs RAM — a big cache is healthy, not a problem.

Force it: asks for Touch ID / password

Real usage? Quit the hogs in the Top Memory panel — their resident + compressed pages both go.
